      House Positions

      Karakas are each house's classical natural significators (naisargika karaka) — the planet(s) whose own domain the house belongs to regardless of this chart's specific placements, e.g. Jupiter for the 5th and 9th, Venus for the 7th. Relationship columns list every directed pair of planets sharing a house, line for line, including pairs involving Rahu or Ketu — the nodes take on Saturn's and Mars's naisargika temperament respectively, a common convention. Each value is read from the maitri table with the first planet as the row and the second as the column — so A→B is how A regards B, and neither column is symmetric.       Influence Framework

      The Influence Engine tab reads each planet and each house in two ways: by the number Shadbala or Bhava Bala gives it, and by the reading factors that number leaves out. This tab documents how that is done: what the tab shows, how the verdict and its colour are reached, which factors are weighed and by how much, how yogas are scored only in their dasha, and what each bala does and does not measure. Every item is Implemented unless marked Open. For a specific chart's verdicts, see the Influence Engine tab itself.

      A note on the BPHS citations below: chapter numbers and names follow Brihat Parasara Hora Sastra, tr. Girish Chand Sharma (Sagar Publications, 2006, two volumes) — cross-checked against that edition's own published table of contents rather than assumed. Chapter numbering is not standardized across published editions: the same "Yoga Karakas" material that is Chapter 36 here is Chapter 34 in the R. Santhanam translation and Chapter 35 in at least one other, so a citation below won't line up chapter-for-chapter with a different edition on your shelf. Treat these as accurate for the Sharma/Sagar edition specifically, not as universal verse-level references. Where this project builds something classical texts don't describe verbatim — the verdict matrix, the factor weights, the house benchmark, the Dispositor Chain's multi-hop walk — that's said plainly rather than attached to a chapter it doesn't have.

      What the Influence Engine tab shows

      Implemented the tab's main content

      The tab has one dropdown with an entry for every planet, labelled by the house it sits in ("House 10 ↔ Sun"), and an entry for every house nothing occupies ("House 3 (empty)"). Each entry shows a coloured dot and a verdict for the house and, where there is one, the planet.

      Choosing an entry shows, in this order:

      1. The planet's Shadbala narrative — its verdict, what its number rests on, and the factors the number leaves out, split into helping and hurting, each with its weight in brackets.
      2. The house's Bhava Bala narrative — the same for the house.
      3. Shadbala, qualitative and quantitative — a grid for each of the six balas. Each row gives the qualitative dimension, the chart's actual condition, the calculation, the Virupas, and the component on the right.
      4. The planet's reading factors not covered by Shadbala or Bhava Bala.
      5. Bhava Bala, qualitative and quantitative — a grid for each of its five components.
      6. House-reading factors Bhava Bala doesn't cover.

      An empty house shows only the house parts. Rahu and Ketu have no Shadbala, so their entry shows a narrative built from the uncounted factors alone. A hand-placed chart has no Shadbala at all, so the dropdown is disabled for it.

      The South Indian chart's Influences side tab carries the same dropdown and shows only the two narratives for the chosen entry, followed by a notes box for that entry. It follows the dasha period in view in the same way.

      Axis 1 — the number

      Implemented

      Planets

      A planet is graded on its Shadbala total against the classical minimum for that planet: Sun 6.5 rupas, Moon 6.0, Mars 5.0, Mercury 7.0, Jupiter 6.5, Venus 5.5, Saturn 5.0.

      Strong: 1.25× the requirement or more  ·  Adequate: 1.0× to 1.25×  ·  Weak: below 1.0×

      The ratio is graded as displayed, to two decimals. Rahu and Ketu have no Shadbala, so they have no number and their verdict comes from the second axis alone.

      A planet's rank out of seven goes by the same ratio, not by its raw total, as Parashara's Light's "Relative Rank" does. Ranking by total would favour the planets with high requirements: Mercury needs 7 rupas and Mars 5, so Mercury can hold more Virupas than Mars and still be the weaker of the two against its own minimum.

      Houses

      Bhava Bala has no classical minimum. Ranking the twelve houses would call four of them Weak in every chart, however healthy they are, so a house is graded against a benchmark instead:

      House benchmark = the lord's required Shadbala (in Virupas) + 30

      The 30 is the midpoint of Bhava Digbala (0 to 60). Aspects, occupation and the day-night factor are taken as neutral. The same cut-offs apply as for planets. The house's rank out of twelve is still shown after its ratio as context ("Adequate (1.03× its benchmark), Rank 9 of 12"), as a planet's rank out of seven is.

      A known side effect: a house ruled by Mars or Saturn has a low benchmark, because those planets have the lowest requirements. Such a house can grade Strong by the number even when its lord is in poor condition; the second axis is what catches that.

      Malefics that do well in difficult houses

      Implemented in the planet verdicts

      Four classical exceptions replace the plain "sits in a Dusthana" penalty. In each, the planet's nature stays malefic; it is the results that turn favourable.

      1. A natural malefic in the 3rd, 6th or 11th. Malefics do well in the Upachaya (growth) houses. The 6th is both a Dusthana and an Upachaya, so a malefic there is read as overcoming enemies, debt and illness. Rahu and Ketu are always counted as natural malefics.
      2. Saturn in the 8th. Saturn signifies longevity and the 8th is the house of longevity. This helps longevity only, so the penalty is dropped and a note is shown with no points.
      3. Ketu in the 12th. Ketu signifies liberation and the 12th is the house of liberation. The reading is spiritual and not material. The textual root is the Karakamsa teaching; applying it to the 12th from the lagna is a common extension and not the literal text.
      4. Viparita Raja Yoga. A lord of the 6th, 8th or 12th placed in another of those houses cancels its own harm. Phaladeepika names the three forms Harsha, Sarala and Vimala. This is about lordship, not about being a natural malefic.

      When Rahu's and Ketu's own results turn favourable

      Rahu and Ketu stay natural malefics, so their aspects and conjunctions count against whatever they touch. The results a node itself gives can still turn good under these conditions, each a helping factor in the node's verdict:

      1. Yogakaraka. A node in a Kendra linked with a Trikona lord, or in a Trikona linked with a Kendra lord, becomes a yogakaraka (BPHS, Yoga Karakas chapter). The link is a conjunction, an aspect either way, or an exchange. Weight 2.
      2. Shanivat Rahu, Kujavat Ketu. Rahu behaves like Saturn and Ketu like Mars. Where that planet is the yogakaraka for the lagna the node inherits the role (weight 2); where it is a functional benefic without a Dusthana lordship, weight 1. This is classical commentary, not a BPHS verse.
      3. Rahu in an Upachaya house (BPHS Ch. 55 v.3). The 3rd, 6th and 11th are covered by the first exception above; Rahu in the 10th adds weight 1.
      4. Auspicious dasha periods (BPHS Ch. 57 v.16 for Ketu; Ch. 59 v.45 and Ch. 55 for Rahu). A node's own periods turn auspicious when it is exalted, in its own sign (Aquarius for Rahu, Scorpio for Ketu, per Ch. 50 v.42), joined or aspected by a natural benefic, in a natural friend's sign, or conjoined with the yogakaraka. Because this speaks of the node's periods, it scores 1 only while the node is a lord of the period in view, and is listed as dormant otherwise.

      Open This app treats Rahu as co-lord of Aquarius and Virgo, and Ketu as co-lord of Scorpio and Pisces. A node can therefore be credited with a Viparita Raja Yoga, or with being the lagna lord, which the classical single-lord reading would not give it.

      The same rule on the house side

      A natural malefic sitting in an Upachaya house helps that house too, with weight 1 for each such occupant, and Rahu or Ketu there is not counted as hurting it. For houses the 10th is included along with the 3rd, 6th and 11th. A planet in the 10th already has its Kendra placement counted in its own Shadbala, which is why the planet-side exception leaves the 10th out; a house has no such credit, and the occupation factor has already taken 60 Virupas from it for each of the Sun, Mars or Saturn. The Saturn, Ketu and Viparita Raja Yoga exceptions remain planet-only.

      Yogas score only in their dasha

      Implemented the verdicts and the network arrowheads follow the period selected on the Dashas tab

      A yoga is a promise in the birth chart. Its results are delivered mainly in the Mahadasha, Antardasha or Pratyantardasha of the planet that forms it. Every other factor in the verdict is a standing condition that holds at all times.

      • The period in view is whatever is selected in the Dashas tab's tree. When a chart is generated the tree opens on the deepest period running today, so the first verdicts are "as of today".
      • A planet is active when it is the MD, AD or PD lord of the period in view. Selecting only a Mahadasha makes just that one lord active.
      • Viparita Raja Yoga scores 2 when the planet is active and 0 when it is dormant. It stays listed either way, marked "active" or "dormant until its dasha".
      • A node's auspicious dasha periods score 1 when Rahu or Ketu is active and 0 when it is dormant.
      • Ishta and Kashta Bala say whether a planet's own periods lean auspicious or difficult. They are shown, not scored: a line right under the planet's Shadbala ratio reads, for example, "Ishta (50.6) > Kashta (9.4)". The line is muted while the planet is dormant and turns the yogas' green while it is an MD, AD or PD lord of the period in view; its tooltip names the lean (auspicious or difficult when one leads by 10 Virupas) and the period. Rahu and Ketu have no Ishta or Kashta Bala.
      • Neecha Bhanga is part condition and part yoga. The cancelled weakness always scores 1; the Raja Yoga adds a second point only when the planet is active.
      • Selecting another period recomputes every verdict on the Influence Engine tab, updates the dropdown, and recolours the network arrowheads. The line above the narratives names the period in view.

      A planet's badge is therefore a reading for the period in view, not a fixed grade of the birth chart. With no dasha available, every yoga is scored as dormant.

      How the narrative sentences are chosen

      Implemented

      The planet narrative names a component as a source of strength when it scores 45 of its 60 Virupas or more, and as something holding the planet back when it scores 15 or less. This covers Uchcha, Dig, Natonnata, Paksha, Ayana and Chesta Bala. A few are named on their own terms: a Moolatrikona or own sign in the rasi, a Kendra placement, being lord of the third of the day, and being lord of the birth year, month, weekday or hora. Drik Bala is named at +20 or more, or −10 or less, with the planet whose aspect contributes most.

      The house narrative states the total, the ratio to the benchmark, the lord's contribution, the sign-type score, the net of aspects with the largest single contributor, and the occupation and day-night factors when they are not zero.

      Each sentence is shown on its own line.

      Network arrowheads

      Implemented on the South Indian chart's dispositor and aspect network

      An arrowhead takes the colour of the verdict of the planet driving that edge: green, gold for amber, or red. The edge's tooltip names the verdict. Because yogas score only in their dasha, the arrowheads change when another period is selected on the Dashas tab. The line's own colour still shows the type of relationship.

      Known gaps

      1. Open Chesta Bala is fitted to Parashara's Light, not derived from a known rule. Over fifteen reference dates Mars, Jupiter, Venus and Saturn agree to about 1 Virupa; Mercury is typically within 3–4 and at worst about 7. See the Shadbala section below.
      2. Open Bhava Drishti on the 12th house of the Siva chart reads 15.7 against Parashara's Light's 22. The difference would close if Mars had no special aspect between 60° and 90°; one data point is not enough to change the aspect table.
      3. Open Ashtakavarga points and a house's condition in the divisional charts are not computed.
      4. Open Counting from the karaka, such as the 10th from the Sun, is not among the house factors.
      5. Open The house benchmark, the verdict matrix and the weights are this project's own. They are named constants in the code and can be tuned.

      The one-hop rule — why this stays acyclic

      Implemented as a settled design rule, followed by the verdicts above and the Dispositor Chain below

      The clean version of "a planet's tone depends on its dispositor's tone" breaks whenever two planets are in mutual exchange — each is the other's dispositor, so a verdict that depended on another planet's already-finished verdict would have no base case there: it's a two-planet loop, not a tree.

      The fix, now applied everywhere this app reasons about influence flowing between planets: every verdict pulls only raw facts, one hop out — never another planet's synthesized verdict. The uncounted factors above keep to this: a planet is scored on its dispositor's dignity and house, never on its dispositor's verdict. It is also why the Dispositor Chain below is purely descriptive and does not feed back into a score.

      The same rule governs the arrowheads of a hand-placed chart, which has no Shadbala to grade. There the arrow tone reads the driving planet's natural benefic or malefic status, its functional lordship (Trikona pulls toward benefic, Dusthana toward malefic, per BPHS Ch. 36, Yoga Karakas) and the nature of any co-tenant, with a split read shown as a gold arrowhead.

      Dispositor Chain

      Implemented — its own panel on the Influence Engine tab

      Classical texts describe a bhava lord's placement one hop at a time (BPHS Ch. 27, Effects of the Bhava Lords) — nothing in BPHS chains that reasoning across multiple hops the way this does. The Dispositor Chain is this project's own extension, built from that classical one-hop fact: it follows a planet from its occupied sign to that sign's lord, then to that planet's occupied sign, and so on — stopping when a planet is reached that rules its own sign, or when a planet already seen in the walk turns up again, reported as a loop rather than an error. Per the one-hop rule above, it never feeds back into a planet's own numeric tone/capacity score — it's read-only, descriptive context.

      The walk uses classical single-lordship (BPHS's SIGN_LORDS) rather than this app's own Rahu/Ketu co-lordship convention — a co-lordship reading would let each node claim two "own" signs, ending a walk through them prematurely in a way the classical reading doesn't. That also means Rahu and Ketu can only ever be a chain's starting planet, never a link partway through: SIGN_LORDS has no entry mapping to either node, since neither classically rules a sign of its own, so a chain can begin at a node but can never arrive back at one mid-walk.

      Ishta/Kashta Bala — the missing Tone number

      Implemented for all seven grahas

      Every tone read in this framework before this was qualitative. Ishta Bala ("auspicious strength") and Kashta Bala ("distress strength") are a classical, computed pair of numbers (BPHS Ch. 30, Ishta and Kashta Balas — 0–60 virupas each, roughly complementary) that fill that gap: Shadbala measures how much power a planet has; Ishta/Kashta measures whether that power tends to express as ease or as friction.

      Ishta Bala  =  (Uchcha Bala + Cheshta) ÷ 2
      Kashta Bala  =  60 − Ishta Bala

      Fixed These follow Parashara's Light, whose seven values for the Siva chart pin the arithmetic down: Ishta is the plain average of Uchcha Bala and a Cheshta figure, and Kashta is what is left of 60. The Cheshta figure is the planet's Chesta Bala, with one exception: for the Sun it is the classical Chesta Kendra, its tropical longitude plus 90° (folded to 0–180°), divided by 3. For the Moon it is its Paksha Bala. The textbook geometric-mean form, √(Uchcha × Cheshta), which this app used before, does not reproduce the reference (it gives the Moon 14.3 where the reference has 25.28).

      The reference's Ishta and Kashta values are known for that one chart only, so the Sun's exception rests on a single figure. For Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus and Saturn the result also inherits whatever error remains in their fitted Chesta Bala, halved.

      On the Influence Engine tab they appear under each planet's number, active in the planet's own periods and dormant otherwise; they do not change its verdict. See "Yogas score only in their dasha" above.

      Dasha timing — dependent vs. independent yogas

      Implemented every yoga on the Influence Engine's Yogas panel now carries a trigger note

      BPHS doesn't use the terms "dasha-dependent" or "dasha-independent" itself, but it draws exactly this distinction through how different yoga chapters describe their own timing. Two chapters state the opposite of each other outright:

      ChapterWhat it says about timing
      Ch. 37, Nabhasha Yogas"Ancestors say that the results due to said (Nabhasha) Yogas will be felt throughout, in all the Dasha periods."
      Ch. 43, Combinations for Wealth"...There is no doubt that these grahas will give wealth during their Dasha periods."
      Ch. 44, Combinations for Penury"A grah associated with one of the lords of Ari, Randhra, and Vyaya Bhava... will in its Dasha periods cause harm to the native's financial aspects."
      Ch. 46, Maraka (Killer) Planets"The major and sub periods of these grahas will bring death on the native... The Dasha of a grah which is an exclusive malefic may also cause death." Closes by pointing to "the chapter related to Dashas" elsewhere in the text.

      The test this yields: a yoga built from an overall chart-shape or count pattern (Nabhasha's 32 combinations, based on how all seven grahas distribute across the houses) is dasha-independent — always running, per Ch. 37's own words. A yoga built from a relationship between one or two specifically named planets — Wealth and Penury's lord-based combinations, Maraka's killer-planet logic — is dasha-dependent: read in the Dasha/Antardasha of the planet(s) that form it.

      Raja Yogas (Ch. 41) and Yogas for Royal Association (Ch. 42) are treated as dasha-dependent by the same test — both are named-lord relationships, the same shape as Wealth and Penury — but neither chapter carries its own explicit "during their Dasha" closing line the way Ch. 43/44 do. That's an extension by analogy, not a direct quote for those two chapters specifically.

      An honest finding, not a design choice: every planet-based yoga this app detects but one — Viparita Raja Yoga, Raja Yoga, Dhana Yoga, Gajakesari Yoga, the other conjunction yogas (Budhaditya, Chandra-Mangala), Neecha Bhanga Yoga, and Parivartana — reduces to one or two specifically named planets. None is a whole-chart distribution pattern the way Nabhasha Yogas are, so by the test above, all of these are dasha-dependent. The remaining one, Pancha Mahapurusha Yoga, also reduces to one named planet — but doesn't fit either bucket cleanly, which is why it gets a third, Hybrid classification below rather than being forced into "dependent."

      Implemented Nabhasha Yogas (dasha-independent) — all four of BPHS's groups (32 yogas), from how the seven grahas (Rahu/Ketu excluded) are distributed:

      1. Ashraya (sign quality): all seven in movable signs — Rajju; fixed — Musala; dual — Nala.
      2. Dala (kendras): natural benefics in three kendras and no natural malefic in any kendra — Mala (Srik); natural malefics in three kendras and no natural benefic in any — Sarpa. The Moon's nature follows its phase, as in Shadbala.
      3. Sankhya (count of occupied signs): 7 Vallaki, 6 Daama, 5 Pasha, 4 Kedara, 3 Shula, 2 Yuga, 1 Gola. Exactly one always applies; BPHS treats it as the fallback when no other Nabhasha Yoga forms, and the Yogas panel says so when another one is present.
      4. Akriti (20 shapes by the houses occupied, counted from the Lagna — same definitions as Varahamihira's Brihat Jataka): Gada (two adjacent kendras), Shakata (1st and 7th), Vihaga (4th and 10th), Shringataka (1, 5, 9), Hala (another trine set), Vajra and Yava (benefics and malefics split between the 1st/7th and 4th/10th), Kamala (all four kendras), Vapi (all panapharas or all apoklimas), Yupa / Shara / Shakti / Danda (four houses from the 1st / 4th / 7th / 10th), Nauka / Kuta / Chhatra / Chapa (seven houses from the 1st / 4th / 7th / 10th), Ardha Chandra (seven houses from a non-kendra), Chakra (six alternate houses from the 1st) and Samudra (from the 2nd). "All planets in houses X" is read as all seven confined to those houses and every one of them occupied — the usual reading, which also keeps the shapes from overlapping. Vajra and Yava are the specific benefic/malefic forms of Kamala, so they replace it when they apply — though as Varahamihira himself noted, they can't physically occur (they need Venus or Mercury 90° from the Sun).

      Checked two ways: every Akriti shape detected correctly on a constructed placement, and a random sample of about 160,000 real charts (1900–2100) never produced two Akriti yogas at once. Akriti yogas are rare in practice — about 1 chart in 100 has one, mostly Yupa, Shara, Shakti or Danda — because Mercury and Venus never stray far from the Sun, which rules out most spread-out shapes.

      The trigger mechanic (Ch. 46's own worked logic)

      Ch. 46 is the one place BPHS actually walks through trigger logic in verse rather than just stating a result, so this app's trigger computation follows that chapter's own mechanic rather than inventing a separate one:

      1. Primary trigger — the yoga-forming planet's own Dasha/Antardasha ("the major and sub periods of these grahas").
      2. Secondary trigger — a planet tied to the primary planet by conjunction or aspect (sambandha) picks up the same triggering power. Ch. 46's own example: "If Rahu or Ketu are placed in Tanu, Yuvati, Randhra, or Vyaya Bhava, or happen to be in the 7th from a Marak lord, or are placed with such a grah, they acquire powers of killing in their major or sub periods."
      3. Suppression — a benefic tie can neutralise the trigger: "He will not, however, do so if Rahu receives a drishti from, or is yuti with a benefic." This app surfaces every sambandha-tied planet as a secondary-trigger candidate rather than silently applying this exception — Ch. 46 states it for Rahu/Ketu specifically; generalising it to every planet is this project's own extension, not a direct quote, so it's left for the reader to weigh rather than baked into the computation as an automatic filter.

      Every yoga on the Influence Engine's Yogas panel now names its primary trigger planet(s) and lists every co-tenant or aspecting planet as a secondary-trigger candidate, computed by this exact mechanic — not a second, invented hierarchy.

      A third category: Hybrid (Pancha Mahapurusha Yoga)

      Implemented — five yogas, one per graha (excluding the luminaries and nodes): Ruchaka (Mars), Bhadra (Mercury), Hamsa (Jupiter), Malavya (Venus), and Sasa (Saturn), each exalted or in its own sign, standing in a Kendra (1st/4th/7th/10th) from the Lagna. These don't sort cleanly into either of the two buckets above: they reduce to one specifically named planet (so not a whole-chart Nabhasha-style pattern — not independent), but the planet's own strong placement is a standing condition from birth, not something waiting on a dasha to exist at all. This app resolves that by tagging them Hybrid: a permanent baseline trait, present continuously, with its peak material expression additionally timed to that planet's own Dasha/Antardasha via the same Ch. 46 mechanic every dasha-dependent yoga already uses. The Yogas panel's Hybrid category sits between Dasha-Independent and Dasha-Dependent for exactly this reason — it borrows a property from each side rather than belonging fully to either.

      No chapter is cited for Pancha Mahapurusha Yoga — this is deliberate, not an oversight. A document reviewed while building this named "Ch. 36" for it; that citation doesn't hold up. Ch. 36 in this edition is confirmed as Yoga Karakas — Pancha Mahapurusha Yoga isn't there. That document's citations turned out to match a different, more widely-circulated translation's numbering (R. Santhanam's) while being labeled as this project's edition — a useful reminder that a citation attached to the right-sounding author and publisher still needs checking against that specific edition's own table of contents, not just against the general shape of the material. Until this project verifies which chapter actually covers it in the Sagar/Sharma edition specifically, this tab says so plainly rather than repeating an unverified number.

      Yoga Bhanga — when a dasha-dependent yoga's delivery is suspended

      Implemented — every yoga's trigger computation now also checks whether the yoga-forming planet's own condition might suspend the yoga's positive delivery during the very period that would otherwise trigger it, rather than presenting every dasha window as equally likely to deliver. Two checks, both reusing facts this app already computes elsewhere rather than adding a new one:

      1. Uncancelled debilitation — the yoga-forming planet is debilitated, and Neecha Bhanga (the same cancellation check the planet verdict and the Neecha Bhanga Yoga scan use) doesn't rescue it.
      2. Sub-minimum Shadbala — the same classical minimum the planet verdict grades against, read here as a caution on delivery rather than a score.

      When either holds, the yoga's trigger note adds a "Yoga Bhanga caution" naming which check fired, rather than silently listing the dasha window as if it were guaranteed to deliver. This is this project's own extension of Ch. 46's trigger mechanic to a second, related question (not just when does a yoga fire, but whether its own condition lets it fire cleanly) — worth stating plainly rather than presenting as a direct quote, the same way the benefic-suppression exception above is.

      Shadbala — Six-Fold Planetary Strength

      The Strength tab's own numbers, cross-checked component by component against a reference implementation (Parashara's Light 9.0) for Siva's chart — what that check found and fixed, and what's still open, below

      BPHS Ch. 29 (Evaluation of Strengths) lays out six independent sources of strength for each of the seven classical grahas — Sthana (positional), Dig (directional), Kala (temporal), Chesta (motional), Naisargika (natural), and Drik (aspectual) — summed into one Virupa total (60 Virupas = 1 Rupa). A planet above its own prescribed minimum is read as strong enough to deliver its promised results in full; below it, results tend to arrive late, partially, or with more effort than the placement alone would suggest — this is exactly what the planet verdict's first axis and the Yoga Bhanga "sub-minimum Shadbala" caution above both read directly.

      PlanetMinimum required
      Sun6.5 Rupas
      Moon6.0 Rupas
      Mars5.0 Rupas
      Mercury7.0 Rupas
      Jupiter6.5 Rupas
      Venus5.5 Rupas
      Saturn5.0 Rupas

      1. Sthana Bala (positional) — five sub-components

      1. Uchcha Bala — degree-distance from the exact debilitation point, divided by 3; maximum at exact exaltation.
      2. Saptavargaja Bala — dignity value summed across seven divisional charts (D1/D2/D3/D7/D9/ D12/D30). Fixed — used to read exactly 15, 30 and 45 Virupas high for the Sun, Moon and Mercury. The cause: a planet in its Moolatrikona sign was given the Moolatrikona score (45) in every divisional chart, but Moolatrikona is a degree band inside one sign, so it only exists in the Rasi (D1). In the other six vargas that sign now scores like any other — 30 if it's the planet's own sign (Sun in a D2 Leo, Mercury in a D3/D12/D30 Virgo), otherwise by the planet's relationship to the sign's lord (Moon in a D12 Taurus → Venus, a friend, 15). The same applies in the D1 when the planet is in that sign but outside the band. All seven planets now match the reference exactly, and with them the whole of Sthana Bala.
      3. Ojhayugma Bala — odd/even sign and navamsa placement, scored by whether the planet's own gender (Moon/Venus favor even; the rest favor odd) matches.
      4. Kendradi Bala — full strength in a Kendra (1/4/7/10), half in a Panaphara (2/5/8/11), a quarter in an Apoklima (3/6/9/12).
      5. Drekkana Bala — Fixed male grahas (Sun/Mars/Jupiter) are strong in a sign's 1st decan, female (Moon/Venus) in the 2nd, neuter (Mercury/Saturn) in the 3rd. Female and neuter were swapped here until checked directly against the reference's actual numbers rather than a description of the rule.

      2. Dig Bala (directional)

      Degree-distance from each planet's own point of maximum directional strength — Sun/Mars weakest at the IC, Jupiter/Mercury weakest at the Descendant, Moon/Venus weakest at the MC, Saturn weakest at the Ascendant — computed from the chart's own true Ascendant/Midheaven, not a whole-sign approximation. Matched the reference closely with no changes needed.

      3. Kala Bala (temporal) — up to nine sub-components

      1. Nathonnata Bala — day/night strength from the Sun's hour angle.
      2. Paksha Bala — lunar-fortnight strength. Fixed Moon no longer doubles this figure — checked against the reference, Moon uses the exact same formula as any other benefic that fortnight; its own doubled lunar significance belongs to Ishta/Kashta Bala's Paksha input, not here.
      3. Tribhaga Bala — which third of the day or night the moment falls in.
      4. Abda / Masa / Vara / Hora Bala — year/month/weekday/hour lordship. Fixed — the year (Abda) lord used to be the weekday of the most recent solar Aries ingress, which gave Mars for Siva's chart where the reference has Jupiter. Year and month lords now come from the Ahargana — the day count from the start of the Kali Yuga (counted inclusively from Ujjain midnight, day 1 being the Friday it began). The year lord is the weekday lord of the day the current 360-day year began; the month lord, of the day the current 30-day month began. This reproduces the reference's year, month and weekday lords together (Jupiter, Moon, Mars) — two independent matches beyond the weekday, though from one chart only.
      5. Ayana Bala — strength from being north or south of the equator. Fixed The formula is 30 × (1 ± sin L), where L is the planet's tropical longitude: the classical (24° ± kranti) ÷ 48 with the kranti taken from the longitude alone, so the planet's own latitude plays no part. The Sun, Mars, Jupiter and Venus gain in the north, the Moon and Saturn in the south, and Mercury on either side; the Sun is not doubled. This matches the reference to the second decimal for all seven planets on fifteen dates. The earlier form used each planet's true declination, which read the Moon up to 5 Virupas off and a southern Mercury as 0 where the reference gives about 59.

      4. Chesta Bala (motional)

      Fixed Sun and Moon used to return zero here outright — the ordinary seeghrochcha-distance formula genuinely has nothing to measure for either (Sun's apparent motion is too uniform to register as "effort"; Moon has no seeghrochcha at all). Checked against the reference, the classical convention isn't zero — Sun's Chesta Bala equals its own Ayana Bala, Moon's equals its own Paksha Bala, both already computed for Kala Bala just above.

      Fitted For the other five planets the formulas are empirical. They were fitted to Parashara's Light's Chesta Bala on fifteen dates between March 1970 and August 1971, chosen to cover conjunctions, stations, retrogrades and oppositions of all five. Each gives a Chesta Kendra, which is divided by 3:

      PlanetChesta KendraTypical errorWorst of 15
      MarsThe angle between the Sun and Mars's heliocentric longitude1.23.3
      Jupiter, SaturnThe same angle × max(1, 1.18 + 0.58 × v ÷ vmax), where v is the daily motion and vmax the top direct speed (0.2415° and 0.129° a day). The factor is 1.76 at top direct speed, 1.18 at a station and 1 in deep retrograde.0.61.4
      VenusIts mean longitude minus the mean Sun, plus 0.45 × (its true longitude minus the mean Sun)0.51.2
      MercuryIts mean longitude minus the mean Sun, plus 1.6 × its elongation from the Sun3.56.6

      Errors are in Virupas. The earlier formula (the arc between a planet and its seeghrochcha) was off by 6 to 17 on the same dates.

      Open These are not the textbook rule. The classical Chesta Kendra, seeghrochcha minus the average of mean and true longitude, does not reproduce the reference with modern mean elements, with B.V. Raman's tables or with Surya Siddhanta mean longitudes; the mean longitude it would need does not move uniformly from date to date. What Parashara's Light actually computes is still unknown, so the Jupiter and Saturn speed factor and the Mercury and Venus coefficients are curve fits. Mercury is the weakest: its orbit is eccentric enough that a fit on mean longitude alone leaves errors of several Virupas.

      5. Naisargika Bala (natural)

      Fixed, chart-independent constants — Sun 60, Moon 51.43, Venus 42.85, Jupiter 34.28, Mercury 25.71, Mars 17.14, Saturn 8.57 Virupas, in that ranking order every chart shares. Matched the reference exactly.

      6. Drik Bala (aspectual)

      Fixed — was the largest gap in Shadbala (every planet well below the reference, one with its sign flipped). Checked against the reference's detailed breakdown for Siva's chart, all seven planets now match to rounding (largest difference 0.01 Virupa). Three separate problems, each confirmed by the numbers rather than assumed:

      • The graded aspect curve was wrong between 120° and 180°. The classical curve falls as 150 − a over 120–150° and rises as 2 × (a − 150) over 150–180°; the old formula instead kept rising gently from 30 to 60 across that whole span. The Moon's reference figure — which depends on four aspects in exactly that range — only matches with the classical version.
      • Jupiter's and Mercury's aspects count in full. Everything else is quartered (benefics added, malefics subtracted), but these two are added whole — the "divide by 4" suspicion above was half right.
      • Special aspects are graded by angle, not a flat 60 by house. Mars (4th/8th), Jupiter (5th/9th) and Saturn (3rd/10th) each get their own curve inside their special angle ranges, peaking at 60 on the exact aspect. The old version gave a flat 60 to any planet in the special house by sign count, however far off the exact angle. Five of the twelve special ranges are confirmed by this chart (Mars 90–120°, Jupiter 210–240° and 240–270°, Saturn 30–60° and 60–90°); the other seven follow the same shape but still need a second reference chart that exercises them.

      Bhava Bala's aspect term (Bhava Drishti) uses the same curve and weighting, so it changed too — that part isn't checked against a reference yet. Mercury is still always treated as a benefic here; the classical rule makes it malefic when conjoined with a malefic, which doesn't arise in Siva's chart.

      Everything marked Fixed above was found by comparing this app's own detailed Shadbala breakdown, component by component, against Parashara's Light 9.0's for the exact same chart — not by re-reading a description of the classical rule and guessing where this implementation might diverge from it. Four real bugs turned up this way and are fixed as of this pass: Chesta Bala silently zeroing for Sun and Moon, Moon's Paksha Bala doubled when it shouldn't be, Sun's Ayana Bala doubled the same way, and the Drekkana Bala's female/neuter decan assignments swapped. Five more genuine gaps turned up in the same comparison. Drik Bala (the biggest of them), Saptavargaja Bala and the Abda (year) lord are now fixed (above); Ayana Bala is fixed too, from a fifteen-date comparison. One is still marked Open: Chesta Bala for the five non-luminary planets, which is fitted to the reference and not derived.
